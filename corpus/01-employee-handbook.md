# Employee Handbook

**Northstar Technologies | Policy HR-001 | Version 1.0**

> **Fictional training document.** Northstar Technologies, its contacts, portals, and policies are invented for RAG demonstration. This handbook is not legal advice or an employment contract.

## 1. Purpose and scope

### 1.1 Purpose
This handbook introduces Northstar Technologies' workplace expectations, employee resources, and procedures. It is intended as a starting point, not a replacement for the topic-specific policies listed in Section 13. Employees should consult the applicable detailed policy when a question concerns a specific entitlement, deadline, approval, or exception.

### 1.2 Who is covered
Unless a section says otherwise, this handbook applies to Northstar's US-based employees, whether working at an office, remotely, or on an approved hybrid schedule. Contractors, consultants, and agency workers follow their agreements and applicable site/security rules; they do not automatically receive employee benefits or PTO.

### 1.3 Policy precedence
Applicable law and signed agreements take precedence over this handbook where required. A topic-specific policy controls over a general handbook summary. People Operations resolves apparent inconsistencies and publishes clarifications; managers cannot unilaterally waive written policies. State and local requirements may provide greater protections or benefits.

### 1.4 No guarantee of employment
This handbook does not create a contract or guarantee continued employment. Where lawful, employment is at will, meaning either Northstar or the employee may end employment at any time, subject to applicable law and agreements.

## 2. Company overview

### 2.1 Our business
Northstar Technologies develops workflow and analytics software for small and mid-sized businesses. Its headquarters is in Denver, Colorado, and it employs approximately 250 people across engineering, product, customer success, sales, finance, operations, and corporate functions.

### 2.2 Our values
- **Customer clarity:** Explain decisions and commitments in plain language.
- **Ownership:** Take responsibility for outcomes, not just tasks.
- **Security by default:** Protect customer and employee information.
- **Respect:** Treat colleagues and customers professionally.
- **Continuous improvement:** Raise concerns and improve processes without blame.

### 2.3 Working language and communications
English is the primary language for company-wide documentation and meetings. Employees should use accessible, respectful language and summarize decisions in the appropriate team workspace. Confidential information must be shared only in approved systems with authorized recipients.

## 3. Employment categories and records

### 3.1 Employment classifications
Employees are designated full-time or part-time and exempt or nonexempt under applicable wage-and-hour rules. Full-time generally means a regularly scheduled 40-hour workweek; part-time means fewer than 30 scheduled hours per week. Schedules between 30 and 39 hours are evaluated individually by People Operations for benefits eligibility. Exempt/nonexempt classification determines overtime and timekeeping requirements; it does not alone determine PTO eligibility.

### 3.2 Employee records
Employees must keep their legal name, mailing address, emergency contact, tax withholding, and direct deposit information current in the fictional **Northstar People Portal**. Report changes within five business days when practical. Access to personnel files is limited to authorized staff with a legitimate business need.

### 3.3 Probationary periods
Northstar does not impose a universal probationary period. Managers conduct structured check-ins during the first 30, 60, and 90 days; these check-ins do not change employment status or statutory rights.

### 3.4 Employment verification
Direct employment-verification requests to People Operations. Managers should not independently disclose salary, performance, or personnel information to third parties.

## 4. Work schedules and attendance

### 4.1 Standard hours
Most corporate teams coordinate around **9:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. Mountain Time, Monday through Friday**, with reasonable flexibility for time zones and role requirements. The standard full-time schedule is 40 working hours per week, with meal and rest breaks handled according to applicable law. Team coverage hours may differ for customer-facing functions.

### 4.2 Attendance and availability
Employees are expected to attend required meetings, meet agreed deadlines, maintain accurate calendar availability, and notify their manager as soon as possible when an unexpected absence affects work. Employees should not work while on approved PTO except in exceptional circumstances coordinated through People Operations.

### 4.3 Timekeeping and overtime
Nonexempt employees must record all hours worked in the People Portal daily, including work performed remotely. Off-the-clock work is prohibited. Obtain manager approval before planned overtime; all hours actually worked must still be reported and paid as required by law. Exempt employees should record absences as directed but do not submit hourly timecards unless instructed.

### 4.4 Breaks
Employees should take required meal and rest breaks. Specific duration and timing follow the law of the employee's work location. Questions about local requirements go to People Operations.

## 5. Workplace locations and remote work

### 5.1 Hybrid and remote arrangements
Some roles are office-based, hybrid, or fully remote. A manager and People Operations must approve a permanent change in work arrangement. Hybrid employees ordinarily attend their assigned office on **Tuesday and Thursday**, unless their team's approved schedule differs. Working from another state or country requires prior written approval because of tax, security, employment, and customer-contract considerations.

### 5.2 Remote-work standards
Employees working remotely must maintain a safe, reasonably private workspace, a reliable internet connection, and availability during agreed collaboration hours. Work-related records must remain in company-approved systems; employees may not use shared household accounts or devices for company work.

### 5.3 Office access and visitors
Employees must use their assigned badge and must not lend it to others. Register visitors in advance, escort them in restricted areas, and report lost badges to Facilities and IT promptly. Follow posted emergency and building instructions.

### 5.4 Detailed rules
See **HR-004 Remote Work Policy** for eligibility, location approvals, equipment, and exceptions. See **IT-001 Equipment Policy** and **SEC-001 Data Security Policy** for device and information controls.

## 6. Compensation, payroll, and performance

### 6.1 Payroll
Employees are paid **semi-monthly on the 15th and last calendar day of each month**, or the preceding business day when a pay date falls on a weekend or company holiday. Payroll statements are available in the People Portal. Report suspected errors to Payroll within five business days of discovery; the company will investigate and correct errors as required by law.

### 6.2 Pay changes and bonuses
Compensation adjustments and incentive awards require documented approval under the applicable compensation plan. Verbal statements do not override signed offer letters or approved plans.

### 6.3 Performance conversations
Managers hold quarterly development conversations and an annual performance review. Reviews cover results, collaboration, professional growth, and compliance with company expectations. Employees may request clarification or submit a written response through People Operations.

### 6.4 Professional development
Role-related learning may be supported when budgeted and approved in advance. Training expenses follow **FIN-001 Expense Reimbursement Policy**; participation in optional training does not automatically qualify for reimbursement.

## 7. Paid time off, holidays, and leave

### 7.1 PTO overview
Eligible regular full-time employees receive annual PTO based on tenure: **15 days (120 hours)** for fewer than three completed years of service and **20 days (160 hours)** beginning on the third service anniversary. PTO is tracked in hours and accrued each semi-monthly pay period. Requests normally require at least five business days' notice and manager approval. Carryover is capped at **five days (40 hours)**, subject to applicable law.

**Authoritative source:** **HR-002 Paid Time Off Policy** contains eligibility, accrual calculations, new-hire rules, request timing, balance limits, carryover, separation treatment, and examples. Where a summary here differs from HR-002, consult HR-002 and People Operations.

### 7.2 Company holidays
Northstar observes designated paid company holidays and publishes the annual calendar before the start of each year. Holiday pay and floating-holiday eligibility are addressed in **HR-003 Holiday Policy**. A holiday is not automatically deducted from an employee's PTO balance.

### 7.3 Other leave
Medical, family, parental, bereavement, jury-duty, military, and other protected or company-provided leaves may have separate eligibility and documentation rules. Contact People Operations rather than assuming PTO is required. See **HR-006 Leave of Absence Policy**.

### 7.4 Reporting unexpected absences
Notify your manager as soon as reasonably possible using an approved channel, preferably before your scheduled start. For an absence that may qualify as protected leave, contact People Operations; employees should not disclose detailed medical information to a manager unnecessarily.

## 8. Benefits and employee support

### 8.1 Benefits overview
Northstar offers eligible employees a benefits package that may include medical, dental, and vision coverage; a retirement savings plan; life and disability insurance; and an employee assistance program. Specific eligibility, premiums, enrollment windows, and plan terms are in **BEN-001 Employee Benefits Guide** and the governing plan documents.

### 8.2 Enrollment and changes
New hires receive benefits enrollment instructions during onboarding. Employees should report qualifying life events promptly through the People Portal. Benefit elections and deadlines are governed by the plan and applicable law, not informal statements from a manager.

### 8.3 Confidential support
Employees can contact People Operations privately about benefits access, workplace accommodations, and available support. Sensitive health information is handled with restricted access.

## 9. Expenses, travel, and purchasing

### 9.1 Business expenses
Reasonable, necessary business expenses may be reimbursed when documented and approved under **FIN-001 Expense Reimbursement Policy**. Employees should seek preapproval for travel and purchases when required; a manager's verbal encouragement does not replace the expense workflow.

### 9.2 Receipts and submissions
Submit itemized receipts and a clear business purpose through the fictional **Northstar Expense Portal**. Personal purchases, duplicate claims, and unsupported expenses may be denied, subject to legal reimbursement requirements.

### 9.3 Corporate cards
Company cards are for authorized business use only. Lost cards and suspicious charges must be reported immediately to Finance. Do not share card details or store them in unapproved applications.

## 10. Information security and technology

### 10.1 Acceptable use
Use approved company accounts, devices, and collaboration tools for company work. Do not install unapproved software, disable endpoint protection, or transfer confidential data to personal cloud storage or personal email.

### 10.2 Authentication and access
Multi-factor authentication is required on supported company systems. Employees must use unique credentials and an approved password manager, never share passwords, and promptly report suspected account compromise. Access is granted according to job responsibilities and removed when no longer needed.

### 10.3 Reporting incidents
Report suspected phishing, lost devices, unintended disclosures, or unusual account activity **immediately** through the fictional **IT Service Desk** or to `security@northstar.example`. Do not investigate suspected breaches independently or delete relevant evidence. **SEC-001 Data Security Policy** specifies incident response steps.

### 10.4 AI tools and confidential information
Do not enter confidential customer data, source code, credentials, personnel information, or nonpublic financial data into unapproved external AI tools. Approved tools and permissible data categories are listed in SEC-001; ask Security when uncertain.

## 11. Workplace conduct and reporting

### 11.1 Respectful workplace
Northstar expects respectful interactions, fair treatment, and professional behavior in offices, online meetings, messaging platforms, and company-sponsored events. Harassment, discrimination, bullying, threats, and retaliation are prohibited.

### 11.2 Conflicts of interest
Employees must disclose outside work, financial interests, gifts, or relationships that could compromise—or appear to compromise—objective business judgment. Disclosures go to People Operations or Legal for review.

### 11.3 Reporting concerns
Employees may report workplace concerns to their manager, People Operations, or the fictional confidential channel `ethics@northstar.example`. Employees may bypass their manager, especially if the manager is involved. Northstar prohibits retaliation for good-faith reporting or participation in an investigation.

### 11.4 Investigations and confidentiality
Reports are reviewed promptly and shared only with those who need information to assess and respond. Absolute confidentiality cannot be promised. Employees are expected to cooperate honestly; corrective action depends on the findings and circumstances. See **HR-007 Workplace Conduct Policy**.

## 12. Joining, changing roles, and leaving

### 12.1 Onboarding
New hires complete identity and employment paperwork, required security training, benefits enrollment, and role-specific orientation. Managers arrange initial access and equipment using **HR-005 Onboarding Procedures**.

### 12.2 Internal transfers
Employees interested in an internal role should review eligibility and discuss timing with People Operations. System permissions and equipment needs must be reassessed when responsibilities change.

### 12.3 Departures
Employees should notify their manager and People Operations in writing when resigning. The company coordinates final pay, benefits notices, system access removal, and equipment return in accordance with applicable law. PTO payout is governed by **HR-002** and applicable state/local requirements.

### 12.4 Return of company property
Return laptops, badges, keys, cards, and other company property using the process supplied by IT. Do not erase or retain company records on personal accounts or storage devices.

## 14. Frequently asked questions

### 14.1 Can I work from another state for two weeks?
Not without prior written approval. Submit the proposed dates and location to your manager and People Operations; see HR-004.

### 14.2 Do I need to use PTO for a company holiday?
Ordinarily no. Paid holidays are tracked separately. Check HR-003 for eligibility and schedule exceptions.

### 14.3 Who approves my vacation request?
Your manager approves scheduling requests through the People Portal; People Operations administers balances and exceptions. See HR-002.

### 14.4 What if I click a suspicious link?
Report it immediately to the IT Service Desk or Security, even if you are unsure whether data was exposed. See SEC-001.

### 14.5 Can I expense a new home-office monitor?
Do not assume reimbursement. Obtain approval under IT-001 and FIN-001 before purchasing.

### 14.6 Can my manager change my PTO accrual rate?
No. PTO accrual follows HR-002 and applicable law. People Operations reviews eligibility and corrections.
