# Paid Time Off Policy

**Northstar Technologies | Policy HR-002 | Version 1.0**

> **Fictional training document.** This policy is invented for RAG evaluation and does not establish actual legal entitlements. Applicable state and local law overrides a conflicting rule, including rules on accrual, carryover, paid sick leave, and payout.

## 1. Purpose and authority

### 1.1 Purpose
Northstar provides paid time off (PTO) so eligible employees can rest, attend to personal needs, and take planned time away from work. This policy defines eligibility, accrual, scheduling, carryover, and separation treatment.

### 1.2 Relationship to other policies
**HR-001 Employee Handbook** provides general workplace context. **HR-003 Holiday Policy** covers paid holidays and floating holidays. **HR-006 Leave of Absence Policy** covers protected and extended leave. PTO is not a substitute for statutory leave rights, and a holiday is not automatically charged to PTO.

### 1.3 Authority and exceptions
People Operations administers balances and interprets this policy. Managers approve time away based on staffing but cannot change accrual rates, waive legal requirements, or promise extra PTO. Exceptions require written approval from People Operations and must comply with applicable law.

## 2. Eligibility

### 2.1 Eligible employees
Regular US-based full-time employees scheduled for **40 hours per week** are eligible for PTO starting on their first day of employment. Employees regularly scheduled for **30–39 hours** are evaluated by People Operations and, if designated PTO-eligible, receive a prorated allowance based on their standard scheduled hours divided by 40. This designation is recorded in the People Portal.

### 2.2 Employees not covered by this plan
Regular part-time employees scheduled for fewer than 30 hours, interns, contractors, and temporary agency workers do not participate in this company PTO plan unless their written agreement or applicable law requires otherwise. They may still have separate paid-sick-leave or other statutory entitlements; contact People Operations for the rules in their work location.

### 2.3 New hires and service dates
PTO begins accruing on the hire date. An employee's service anniversary is the same month and day as the hire date; People Operations adjusts leap-day anniversaries to February 28 in non-leap years for this plan. Prior contractor service does not count toward the PTO tenure tier unless People Operations documents an exception.

## 3. Annual allowance and accrual

### 3.1 Full-time PTO tiers

| Completed service | Annual PTO allowance | Equivalent hours |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Less than 3 years | 15 workdays | 120 hours |
| 3 years or more | 20 workdays | 160 hours |

A workday means eight hours for a standard 40-hour, five-day schedule. PTO balances and requests are recorded in **hours**, not fractions of calendar days.

### 3.2 Semi-monthly accrual
Northstar has **24 semi-monthly payroll periods** per calendar year. For an employee eligible for a full year at the same tier and schedule, the standard credit is:

- 120 hours / 24 = **5.0000 hours per payroll period** for the 15-day tier.
- 160 hours / 24 = **6.6667 hours per payroll period** for the 20-day tier, with an annual true-up so total regular annual accrual is exactly 160 hours.

Credits post at the close of each pay period, subject to any lawful balance cap. The People Portal displays balances to two decimal places; Payroll maintains greater internal precision and adjusts the final annual posting to prevent rounding drift.

### 3.3 Partial periods and midyear changes
For a new hire, separation, or schedule change within a pay period, the credit is prorated by **eligible calendar days in that pay period divided by total calendar days in that pay period**. A tenure-tier change on the third anniversary is calculated separately for the days before and after the anniversary. People Operations handles these adjustments in the People Portal.

### 3.4 Prorated schedules
An approved PTO-eligible employee scheduled for 32 hours per week receives 32/40 = **80%** of the full-time annual hours: 96 hours at the lower tier or 128 hours at the higher tier for a complete year. PTO requests reflect the hours the employee was otherwise scheduled to work.

### 3.5 Balance cap
The normal PTO balance cap is **200 hours** for a full-time employee; for a prorated eligible schedule, the cap is scaled by the same scheduled-hours ratio. Accrual pauses when the balance reaches the cap and resumes when the balance falls below it. Northstar will apply any more protective accrual or cap rule required by law. Accrual not posted because of a lawful cap is not retroactively credited.

## 4. Using PTO

### 4.1 Permitted uses
PTO may be used for vacations, personal appointments, family responsibilities, rest, and other personal needs. Employees do not need to provide private details for ordinary PTO requests. When an absence may qualify for protected leave or legally required sick time, People Operations will help identify the applicable policy and legal rights.

### 4.2 Request increments
Employees may request PTO in **one-hour increments**, or smaller increments where required by law or an approved accommodation. An employee should request only the hours they would otherwise have worked.

### 4.3 Advance notice
For planned absences, submit a request in the **Northstar People Portal at least five business days before the first day off**. A business day means Monday through Friday excluding Northstar company holidays. For an absence longer than five consecutive workdays, employees are encouraged to give at least 20 business days' notice, but this is a planning recommendation, not an additional mandatory minimum.

### 4.4 Approval workflow
1. Employee checks the projected PTO balance and team calendar.
2. Employee submits dates, hours, and optional coverage notes in the People Portal.
3. The system routes the request to the direct manager.
4. The manager approves or denies based on coverage, workload, and equitable scheduling, normally within **three business days**.
5. The portal notifies the employee and updates the projected balance. Actual PTO is deducted when the absence is processed.

A submitted request is **not approved** until the portal shows an approved status. Managers should explain denials and work with employees on alternatives. Approval must not interfere with legally protected leave.

### 4.5 Competing requests and peak periods
Managers consider business coverage, prior commitments, fairness, and the order requests were received. No team has an automatic company-wide blackout period. A team proposing a temporary coverage restriction must obtain People Operations review and communicate it in advance; legally protected absences remain protected.

### 4.6 Unexpected absences
Notify your manager as soon as reasonably possible, preferably before your shift starts. Enter the absence in the People Portal when able. Do not delay reporting a protected or urgent absence to meet the five-business-day planned-request rule. People Operations may route the absence under HR-006 or a legally required sick-leave process.

## 5. Balances, borrowing, and changes

### 5.1 Available versus projected balance
**Available balance** is accrued unused PTO as of the latest processed payroll period. **Projected balance** includes expected future accrual through the requested date and subtracts approved future PTO. A projected balance is an estimate, not a guarantee.

### 5.2 Negative balances
PTO may not ordinarily be used before it is accrued. A manager cannot approve a negative balance. People Operations may approve a documented exception of up to **16 hours** of advanced PTO for an eligible employee after reviewing the circumstances; repayment or final-pay deductions are permitted only where lawful and separately authorized when required.

### 5.3 Cancelling or changing PTO
Employees may cancel or edit future approved requests in the People Portal. Changes to dates or hours are routed back to the manager for approval. If plans change after an absence begins, notify the manager and People Operations promptly so records can be corrected.

### 5.4 Holidays during PTO
If an eligible employee's approved PTO overlaps an observed paid company holiday, the holiday is generally recorded as holiday pay rather than PTO for the employee's normally scheduled hours. See HR-003 for eligibility and observed dates. Example: a five-day vacation containing one eight-hour paid holiday ordinarily uses **32 PTO hours**, not 40.

## 6. Year-end carryover and forfeiture

### 6.1 Standard carryover rule
At the transition from December 31 to January 1, employees may carry over up to **40 unused PTO hours (five standard workdays)**. Under the fictional default company plan, unused hours above 40 expire after year-end processing **only where lawful**. In jurisdictions where accrued vacation cannot lawfully be forfeited, People Operations applies the required alternative and documents it in the employee's local policy addendum.

### 6.2 Carryover and new-year accrual
Carried hours remain part of the employee's PTO balance and count toward the 200-hour normal balance cap. New-year accrual posts on the usual payroll schedule; carryover is not a separate annual grant.

### 6.3 Example: year-end balance
An employee has 56 unused PTO hours on December 31. Under the default rule, **40 hours carry into January** and **16 hours expire**, provided the employee's jurisdiction permits forfeiture. In a jurisdiction prohibiting forfeiture, the local legal rule controls and the 16 hours must not simply be removed.

### 6.4 No cash-out election
Employees cannot elect to exchange unused PTO for cash while actively employed under the standard plan. Mandatory payments required by law or a written approved agreement are exceptions.

## 7. Leave interactions and special situations

### 7.1 Paid holidays
Company holidays are separate from PTO and governed by HR-003. An employee who is ineligible for holiday pay should ask People Operations how the day is recorded rather than assume it is automatically PTO.

### 7.2 Protected leave and sick time
Medical, family, parental, disability-related, military, and other protected absences may have distinct eligibility, job-protection, notice, and pay rules. People Operations determines whether PTO may or must run concurrently with a leave, consistent with applicable law. Employees are not required to disclose diagnoses to their manager to request ordinary PTO.

### 7.3 Accrual during leave
PTO accrues during paid working time and paid PTO under the standard plan. During an **unpaid leave**, PTO accrual is generally suspended for the unpaid days, unless applicable law or a written leave arrangement requires otherwise. People Operations calculates prorated pay-period credits when a leave begins or ends midperiod.

### 7.4 Company closures
A scheduled company holiday follows HR-003. For an unscheduled office closure, People Operations and Payroll communicate whether affected employees are paid, work remotely, or use another approved status. Employees should not self-charge PTO without instructions.

## 8. Separation and payout

### 8.1 Final balance reconciliation
On separation, People Operations reconciles accrued hours, PTO used, approved adjustments, and local-law requirements through the final date of employment. Future projected accrual after separation is not earned.

### 8.2 Unused PTO payout
**Unused accrued PTO is paid out at separation where required by applicable law or a written agreement.** Under the fictional default plan, there is no discretionary payout in jurisdictions that do not require it. People Operations must review the employee's work location before finalizing any payout; managers must not promise that PTO will or will not be paid.

### 8.3 Negative balances at separation
If an employee has an authorized advanced-PTO deficit, Payroll may recover it only when permitted by applicable law and any required written authorization. Otherwise Northstar absorbs the deficit.

### 8.4 PTO during notice periods
Employees may request PTO during a resignation notice period, but approval depends on operational coverage and legal obligations. PTO does not automatically extend the employment end date. Protected leave rights remain unaffected.

## 9. Roles, records, and escalation

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Employee | Submit accurate requests, check balances, communicate absences |
| Manager | Review requests promptly and fairly, coordinate coverage |
| People Operations | Determine eligibility, administer exceptions, interpret local rules |
| Payroll | Post accruals, reconcile balances, process lawful final payments |

### 9.1 Record corrections
If an employee believes a PTO balance is incorrect, they should email `people@northstar.example` with the pay period, dates, and discrepancy. People Operations acknowledges the inquiry within **two business days** and aims to resolve straightforward corrections within **ten business days**. Complex local-law questions may take longer; the employee will receive an update.

### 9.2 Appeals of scheduling decisions
An employee who disagrees with a denied PTO request should first discuss alternatives with the manager. If the employee believes the decision is inconsistent, discriminatory, retaliatory, or contrary to protected leave rights, they may contact People Operations directly or report through `ethics@northstar.example`.

### 9.3 Data handling
PTO balances and absence reasons are personnel data. Managers should access only information needed for scheduling. Medical documentation, if needed for another leave process, must be sent to People Operations through approved secure channels, not posted in team chats.

## 10. Worked examples

### 10.1 Employee with two years of service
**Question:** How much PTO does a full-time employee receive after two years?  
**Answer:** 120 hours (15 eight-hour days) annually, accruing at 5 hours per semi-monthly payroll period for a complete year at the lower tier.

### 10.2 Third service anniversary
**Question:** What happens when an employee reaches three years?  
**Answer:** Their annualized rate changes from 120 to 160 hours beginning on the third anniversary. If the anniversary falls mid-pay-period, that period's accrual is prorated between the two rates; there is no immediate lump-sum grant of 40 extra hours.

### 10.3 Four-day vacation request
**Question:** How many hours should an employee request for four scheduled eight-hour days?  
**Answer:** 32 PTO hours, assuming none of those days is an observed paid holiday.

### 10.4 Request made two days ahead
**Question:** Can an employee request vacation with only two business days' notice?  
**Answer:** They can submit the request, but it does not meet the normal five-business-day planned-absence requirement. The manager and People Operations may consider an exception. Urgent or protected absences are handled separately.

### 10.5 Request exceeds accrued balance
**Question:** Can someone with 10 accrued hours take 24 hours of PTO next week?  
**Answer:** Not ordinarily. They need enough accrued hours when the absence is processed or a written People Operations exception for advanced PTO, capped at 16 hours under the standard policy. A manager alone cannot authorize a negative balance.

### 10.6 Carryover in a restrictive jurisdiction
**Question:** Will 70 unused hours always be reduced to 40 at year-end?  
**Answer:** No. The standard 40-hour carryover cap applies only where lawful. People Operations must apply the employee's jurisdiction-specific requirements before reducing any accrued balance.

### 10.7 PTO and parental leave
**Question:** Does parental leave automatically use PTO?  
**Answer:** No universal rule applies. People Operations reviews HR-006, the employee's circumstances, and applicable law to determine pay coordination and any concurrent use.

## 11. Frequently asked questions

### 11.1 Is PTO front-loaded on January 1?
No. PTO accrues over the year in semi-monthly pay periods. Carryover from the prior year may be available at the start of the year.

### 11.2 Does a weekend count as PTO?
Not if the employee was not scheduled to work. PTO is deducted only for scheduled work hours missed.

### 11.3 Who can see my reason for taking PTO?
Managers see scheduling information necessary to approve an absence. Employees do not need to include sensitive personal or medical details in ordinary PTO requests.

### 11.4 Can a manager deny a vacation request?
Yes, for legitimate scheduling or coverage reasons, subject to the fair-review standards in Section 4 and applicable protected-leave laws.

### 11.5 Can I carry over more than five days?
Under the default policy, no; however, more protective state or local law or a documented written exception may apply.

### 11.6 What if I move to a different state?
Notify People Operations before the move. Your work location may change the legal rules governing accrual, carryover, paid sick leave, and payout. Remote work location approval is separately governed by HR-004.
